Who We Are

We are the Balloon Payload Program, or BPP for short, a student organization at the University of Maryland and a program primarily funded by the Maryland Space Grant Consortium.

To provide easy, affordable access to near-space, we fly student-created payloads on high-altitude balloons to upwards of 100,000 feet (>30 km and ~3x the cruising altitude of most airliners), in order to accomplish each of their scientific / engineering goals. We fly 3 flights per school semester and 1-2 flights during the summer, each with 6-10 payloads.

So, if you are interested in learning more about the payload-building / high-altitude balloon launching process or in building a payload and flying it with us, please reach out to us at ssl-nearspace@umd.edu. Also, check out our Instagram, umdnearspace, and our YouTube channel, @umdnearspace, for pictures and videos from our launches.

What We Do

Development

  • Mechanical design / assembly
  • 3D printing
  • Programming
  • Electronic design
  • Soldering
  • PCB design / fabrication
  • Radio communications design

Launch

  • Weather / balloon trajectory predictions
  • Helium tank handling, pressure regulation, and balloon inflation
  • Tying payloads onto the flightline
  • Directing personnel and the launch overall

Flight

  • Typically 1.5 to 2+ hours in-flight
  • Altitudes up to more than 100,000 feet (30 km)
  • Atmospheric pressure down to about 1% of sea level
  • Temperatures down to -70°F (-56.7°C)
  • Ground speeds up to 173 mph (278 kph)

Recovery

  • Payloads are recovered on the same day if possible
  • A team drives out to the landing site with special equipment for recovery
  • A professional tree climber is called to recover the payloads if they land in a tree out of reach
